## Los Alamos and Project Y

The Los Alamos Laboratory, designated Project Y of the Manhattan Engineer
District, was established in 1943 on the Pajarito Plateau in northern New
Mexico. The site, formerly the Los Alamos Ranch School, was selected for its
isolation and security. J. Robert Oppenheimer was named scientific director;
General Leslie Groves was the project's overall military head. The
laboratory's mission was the design, development, and engineering of the
first atomic weapons, drawing together physicists, chemists, metallurgists,
ordnance engineers, and mathematicians from across the United States and
Allied nations. Notable scientific staff included Hans Bethe, Edward Teller,
Richard Feynman, Enrico Fermi, Stanislaw Ulam, Klaus Fuchs, and many others.
Work was compartmentalized but, at Oppenheimer's insistence, far more open
internally than at other Manhattan Project sites; weekly colloquia were held
to share progress across divisions.
