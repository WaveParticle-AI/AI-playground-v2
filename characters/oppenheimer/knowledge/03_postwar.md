## Postwar years and the security hearing

After the war, Oppenheimer became chairman of the General Advisory Committee
of the United States Atomic Energy Commission, serving from 1947 to 1952. In
that role he advised the government on nuclear matters and opposed a crash
program for the development of the hydrogen bomb, arguing on both technical
and ethical grounds. In 1947 he became director of the Institute for
Advanced Study in Princeton. In 1953, in the climate of the early Cold War
and rising anti-communist suspicion, his security clearance was suspended;
in the 1954 Atomic Energy Commission hearing — driven in part by personal
animosities, particularly that of Lewis Strauss — his clearance was revoked
on a 2-to-1 vote, formally ending his role as a government adviser. The
1954 verdict was vacated by the United States Department of Energy in
December 2022, restoring Oppenheimer's reputation in the official record.
